## What they say
- StS has no tutorial level: the onboarding is "content-shaped". One class unlocked, 10-card deck with exactly two verbs (Strike, Defend) plus one card that applies a debuff, the first three fights come from a weaker pool, and a safer blessing menu. Teaching order: intent, block, energy, deck edit, map risk, relics. Draw/exhaust/powers are withheld.
- Inscryption's first match is a guided, high-stakes tutorial; you are expected to lose and learn from the replay. Mechanics (bones, etc.) are introduced after your first death (Ludo Guide).
- Game Accessibility Guidelines: basic = "Include interactive tutorials"; intermediate = a way to practise without failure, contextual help, indicate/remind controls.
- Cavanagh: with ~11 cards things get "pretty confusing", so push reward complexity to later floors.

## Takeaways for Free Nassau
1. First-run interactive tutorial ("Maiden voyage"): scripted three-fight prologue on a tiny deck, one prompt at a time, each requiring the action: (1) read the enemy intent and play Tack Hard; (2) fire Broadside; (3) End Turn; (4) the ship is crippled: choose Grapple and board; (5) Cutlasses kill crew; (6) hire an officer; (7) sail home. Can be skipped and replayed from Settings ("Quartermaster's briefing").
2. The first three fights of Act I come from the easy pool, and the first reward screen offers 3 simple cards. Hold back Burn, Weak, officers' cards, and events until after the first win or the tutorial.
3. Contextual one-time tips (dismissible, stored in meta): first time phase changes, first injured officer, first extraction point, first Notoriety.
4. A "Rules" page (Captain's Manual) in the menu lists every keyword with its icon. Tap any keyword chip anywhere to read it.

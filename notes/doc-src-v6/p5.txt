# 5. Critiques and Prioritized Roadmap
These critiques drove the v6 plan (section 4). Section 4.2 maps each one to a change; the status column of the roadmap table in 5.3 is updated when v6 ships.
## 5.1 Player Critique (Gamer's-Eye Review)
A gamer's-eye review, written as a player who wants a finished product rather than a prototype. Each point is a bullet with the one-line fix that would address it. Written against the v5 build.
• Looks like a web page, not a game. Vector ships, tray and props sit next to painted art and read as prototype, and very little moves except cards. What would fix it: replace the vector props with painted layers and add ambient motion (sea, flags, smoke) so every screen feels alive.
• Battles don't show enough. The screen shows numbers and bars, with no big cannon volley or boarding charge, and hits don't feel powerful. What would fix it: add full-screen volley, impact and boarding-charge sequences with screen shake, hit-stop and big damage numbers.
• Art repeats. Several enemies share one painting, and every card uses the same small etched icon style; card art is the biggest gap against Slay the Spire and Inscryption. What would fix it: commission unique illustrations for every enemy and card, starting with the 20 most-played cards.
• Thin content. There are few captains and a limited set of cards and events, and about 60% of runs end at the same final boss, so replay value needs variety. What would fix it: grow the pool (more captains, cards and events, plus a second Act III boss) so each run feels different.
• Cards feel small on a phone. Reading a card means tapping it and looking at a separate panel, and big hands collapse into a grid. What would fix it: print the key number on the card face and enlarge the touched card in a fanned hand.
• Runs are long and tap-heavy. Select-then-confirm is safe but slow. What would fix it: a speed-up toggle, skippable animations, one-tap play for experienced players and shorter boss fights.
• Sound and music. There is no music, only synthesized beeps and thuds. What would fix it: a looping shanty-style score for each act plus recorded cannon, rigging and sea effects.
• No tutorial or onboarding. Ship phase versus boarding, officers and sailing home all have to be discovered by trial and error. What would fix it: a short guided first voyage that teaches each of them in turn.
• Progress feels invisible. Notoriety, unlocks and the Nassau base are not presented as exciting rewards. What would fix it: a post-run reveal that shows Nassau growing, celebrates each unlock and keeps a visible progress track.
Top 3 highest-payoff fixes: (1) combat feedback and animation, (2) unique art for cards and enemies, (3) a short tutorial.
## 5.2 Round 2: Harder Criticisms
The first nine points are what a player sees. These twelve are harder: I re-read the code and the notes, played the live build in a headless 390x844 browser, and ran 240 bot voyages on the current build (60 per base captain, Notoriety 0, 29% wins) plus two variant sims (never take a card reward; always take the middle lane). The bot is a modest player (it takes the first card offered and picks events at random), so treat the numbers as directional evidence about the design, not proof about human play. Estimates are marked. What held up: the title screen loads in about 2.8 seconds with 407 KB, a reload in the middle of combat resumes the same hand, safe-area insets are used and a Reduce motion toggle exists. Severity is High, Medium or Low. Each point gives the evidence, then a suggested fix.
• R2-1 [High] Acts I and II are a toll road; the run is decided at Hornigold. Evidence: only 22 of 240 runs (9%) died before Act III (6 in Act I, 16 in Act II), while 208 (87%) reached Hornigold with a median 81% captain HP and 79% hull and 149 runs (62%) died in Act III, 139 of them to Hornigold (68 in the duel, 61 in the ship phase, 10 while boarding). The state at the Act I boss does not predict the result (28% wins above median HP, 30% below), so early choices barely matter; the state at Hornigold does (47% above median HP, 19% below). A player feels ten safe fights and then a wall. Suggested fix: give Acts I and II real stakes (an elite that costs resources, ration pressure, a pursuit clock), and flatten the cliff by shortening the Hornigold duel and adding a checkpoint between his ship phase and the duel.
• R2-2 [High] Deck building is a weak decision; the ship tier is the real lever. Evidence: a bot that skips every card reward still wins 23% (54 of 240) against 29% (69 of 240) when it takes the first card offered, so the whole card economy is worth about 6 points. The final ship tier tracks results far more: periagua 11% (10 of 92), sloop 31% (24 of 78), brigantine 45% (17 of 38), snow 56% (18 of 32). That is a correlation, but it points to "buy the next hull" as the solved strategy. 14 of 32 cards involve Block and 22 of 32 cost 1 energy, so a card pick rarely changes a turn. Suggested fix: make cards build-arounds (powder, burn, boarding and officer synergies), add card removal or upgrade at ports, and give ship upgrades a trade-off (crew upkeep, slower sailing) instead of strictly better.
• R2-3 [Medium] Phase-locked cards create dead draws. Evidence: in the ship phase every Crew card is unplayable and in boarding every Ship card is. In the opening Blackbeard hand I captured, 3 of 6 cards were grey. Blackbeard starts with 6 Ship, 3 Crew and 1 Captain cards (Bellamy and Vane 5, 4, 1). A fight that ends by sinking never uses a Crew card at all, so a crew-heavy reward can be a dead pick, and the single swap per turn is the only relief. Suggested fix: let a locked card do a smaller version of its job in the wrong phase (Cutlasses give 3 Block at sea, a Swivel Gun covers boarding fire), or draw only playable cards on the first hand.
• R2-4 [Medium] Enemies are scripted loops with only the next move visible: memorizable, not plannable. Evidence: each ship enemy cycles a fixed list and ignores what you do (the Rival Pirate Sloop plays Broadside, Grapeshot, Broadside, Brace, Aimed Shot, then repeats, with base damage 7, 6, 5, 5, 5 scaled up per act; the Hornigold duel is Slash, Slash, Guard, Lunge). The v3 pass removed the pattern strip, so you see only the next intent: a veteran has the loops memorized while a newcomer cannot plan two turns ahead. Inside a fight the only randomness is the draw and a weather modifier of plus or minus 2 on a 10-point attack, which is noise rather than a decision. The luck that matters sits in reward offers and draws. Suggested fix: show the next two intents, give each enemy one reactive rule (it braces when your Block is up, boards when your crew is low), and randomize where each loop starts.
• R2-5 [Medium] The map is a three-lane ladder with shallow route choices. Evidence: each act is 3 columns by 10, 7 and 7 rows, you can only step one column sideways, and most nodes are the same mix (weights: fight 42, event 20, recruit 10, port 9, elite 8, shipwright 4; the first row is always a fight, the middle row a treasure, the last a cove). That is about 27 map screens per run with 2 or 3 buttons each. A bot that ignores the map and always takes the middle lane wins 24% (58 of 240) against 29% with simple route rules, and its Act I deaths jump from 6 to 29, so routing matters in Act I (recruit, port, cove) and almost nowhere else. Suggested fix: widen to 4 or 5 lanes with real forks, put elites and treasure on visible risk-reward branches, and let the player skip a leg by burning rations.
• R2-6 [High] Tap economy: hundreds of taps for a few dozen decisions. Evidence: ten UI-driven playtests in the notes (v3-era build) used 374 to 1,330 taps per run (median about 820, 5 to 15 fights), logging only 13 to 29 decisions each outside combat. Select-then-confirm makes every card play two taps, bot runs average 13.6 fights and about 550 engine steps, and the Hornigold fight alone has run 17 to 22 turns. A full run is probably 30 minutes or more on a phone (estimate, not timed). Suggested fix: one-tap play with an undo (or hold to read), auto end turn when nothing is playable, a 2x speed toggle, and cut Hornigold into two shorter stages.
• R2-7 [Medium] Meta progression is a slow, flat grind with little to unlock. Evidence: Nassau has 3 buildings with 2 levels each, costing 120 and 300 (Powder House), 120 and 300 (Tavern), 150 and 400 (Shipyard), 1,390 silver in total, and each only adds a flat starting bonus (+3 or +6 powder, +6 or +12 crew with rations, +1 Sheathing and Guns). Bot runs bank about 133 silver on average (about 389 per win, about 32 per Act III death, since death banks only 25%), so maxing the base takes about 10 runs, roughly 5 hours at the tap counts above (estimate). The only content unlocks are the Rackham captain and the Notoriety levels; nothing unlocks cards, events or ships. Suggested fix: unlock cards, officers, events and ships through milestones, give a death a guaranteed floor of silver per act reached, and make buildings change options rather than only add stats.
• R2-8 [Medium] The pirate fantasy lives in the menus, not in the turn. Evidence: a combat turn is energy, Block cards and attacks, which is the Slay the Spire template (14 of 32 cards involve Block). The pirate ideas (supplies, officers, extraction, prize ships) sit on the map and menu screens. The captain acts through a passive and two or three cards; there is no command verb such as giving orders, flying a flag, offering terms or splitting plunder, and boarding is a second round of the same cards. Suggested fix: add one captain-only action per turn (Orders: Fire at will, Hard to port, Parley), let reputation change enemy behaviour (a feared merchant strikes her colours), and move prize and plunder choices into combat.
• R2-9 [Medium] Accessibility gaps: zoom locked, tiny labels, small targets, one-handed reach. Evidence: the viewport meta sets maximum-scale=1 and user-scalable=no, which blocks pinch zoom, and there is no text-size setting. The headless audit of the combat screen at 390x844 found 7 text elements at 10.5 to 11px (Draw and Discard labels, the "/78" maxima), four 20x20 tap targets in the top HUD, a 23px-tall weather chip and a 34px enemy bar, all under the usual 44px target. The menu button is top right, the hardest spot for a thumb, while End Turn sits bottom centre (good). Red and blue ink arcs and bars carry meaning but numbers and icons back them up; I have not tested with a colour-blind filter. Reduce motion exists. Suggested fix: allow zoom, set a 12px floor with an A, A+ and A++ text-size toggle, pad small targets, add a left or right hand layout (menu to the bottom), and run a colour-blind filter test.
• R2-10 [Low] Phone resilience: save and resume work, but offline and storage are fragile. Evidence: I reloaded mid-combat after playing a card and Continue restored the same hand and energy, which is good. The gaps: no web manifest and no service worker (it cannot be installed or run offline), fonts come from Google Fonts at runtime, and saves live only in localStorage in a single slot, so clearing site data, or the browser evicting storage for sites not added to the home screen, can erase both the run and the banked Nassau silver. Performance is fine on the title screen (407 KB, 18 requests); art used later in a run was not measured. Suggested fix: add a manifest and a service worker that precaches the art, self-host the fonts, request persistent storage, and offer an export or import save code.
• R2-11 [Medium] Combat is dense and hides its math. Evidence: the combat screen at 390x844 carries about 76 words and numbers: three health tracks, silver, powder, rations, kits, four officer slots, weather, phase, enemy hull and crew, a log, and deck and discard counts. The enemy intent is an icon, a number and tiny ship, crew or heart glyphs; the words ("Grapeshot 6, kills crew") only appear if you tap the badge. Real damage also depends on hidden dials (per-act enemy damage multipliers, weather plus or minus 2, Weak cutting 25%, half damage with no powder), and the first Act I sloop opens with a Broadside 11 against 45 hull, with no cue whether 11 is scary. Suggested fix: print the verb under the intent badge, add an incoming-damage preview ("after Block: 4 hull"), and fold supplies and officers into a tray that expands on tap.
• R2-12 [Medium] There are no wow moments; the biggest beats are text. Evidence: the high points of a run are a boss name on a panel, a trophy line and a text end screen. The Hornigold duel is a portrait and a health bar, sometimes for 17 to 22 turns. A prize ship capture, a first sinking, extraction and the final victory all reuse the same card panel and a reward list. Nothing is authored to make a player screenshot it or tell a friend: no boss entrance, no broadside cinematic, no sailing into Nassau at the end. Suggested fix: author three set pieces: a boss intro card for each act, a one-shot cannon volley and boarding charge for kill moments, and an ending sequence sailing into Nassau with the plunder tally.

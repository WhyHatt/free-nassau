A roguelike deck builder set in the Free Nassau world. Living doc, v1.1 (Oct 14, 2026). Play: https://whyhatt.github.io/free-nassau/
# 1. Vision & Pillars
• Vision: Ren captains the sloop Endurance out of Nassau, with James Kidd at his side, sailing island to island to keep Nassau a free city. Every run is one voyage.
• Pillar: Captain's choices. Every node, card pick and purchase is a real trade-off (risk vs. reward, now vs. later).
• Pillar: Pirate economy. Coin and cargo (silver, timber, food, fresh water, gunpowder, rum, wine, rope) matter, in fights and between them.
• Pillar: Readable fights. Enemies telegraph intents; numbers are small and legible on an iPhone.
• Pillar: Luck you can steer. Dice add swing (Dicey Dungeons style), but relics and cards let you tame it.
• Pillar: Nassau grows. Long-term, runs feed a rebuilding meta-progression for the free city.
• Pillar: Free and portable. A single web page, free hosting, plays on phone and desktop.
# 2. What's Built (v1 → v1.1 → v2)
• Single-page HTML/CSS/JS game, hosted free on GitHub Pages, auto-saves the run in the browser (localStorage).
• Ren: 60 HP, 3 energy per turn, draw 5. Starter deck: 5 Cutlass Strike, 4 Brace, Lucky Shot, Hoist Sails (11 cards). Starts with James's Spyglass relic.
• 20 cards: attacks, blocks and skills; dice cards (Lucky Shot, Powder Keg, Dice of Fortune); Strength, Weak, Vulnerable, Exhaust.
• Enemies: Rival Pirates, Boarding Party, Navy Frigate (elite), Navy Man-o'-War (boss), each with a repeating telegraphed intent pattern.
• Map: 3 columns wide, forward/diagonal moves, ends in the boss.
• Nodes: fight, elite, merchant, tavern rest, random event, treasure (v1.1).
• Relics (v1.1: 7): James's Spyglass, Fair Wind's Colors, Lucky Doubloon, Rum Cask, Tarred Hull, Powder Monkey, Prize Ledger.
• v1.1 changes: ☰ rules/status legend; End Turn as a normal button at the bottom of the page; card rarity (★/★★/★★★) with weighted reward odds; treasure row mid-map; elites guaranteed relic + 4-card pick + heal; merchant with rarity prices, a 50%-off sale card, once-per-visit removal; tavern 'Swap tales' card option; ambush chance on events; 99 starting coin; visual polish.
• v2 rough draft (Oct 14, 2026, commit 6bf1f82, live at whyhatt.github.io/free-nassau): a full rewrite into index.html + game.js + ui.js. Ren and James are gone from the game; a captain placeholder image stands in for art.
• Captain select: Blackbeard (terror: enemy crew -10% when boarding), Bellamy (officers 25% cheaper, +30% loot), Vane (+2 cannon damage), Bonny & Read (+2 damage, +1 energy in duels), each with its own starter deck. "Calico Jack" Rackham unlocks after your first extraction or win. Runs start with only the captain, a basic ship and basic crew, with no officers.
• Three persistent tracks: hull, crew (count + morale) and captain HP. Losing the ship means Abandon Ship (drop to a damaged periagua, lose 40% crew, half your cargo and coin, an officer injured). Only the captain's death ends the run.
• Two-phase battles from one deck tagged Ship / Crew / Captain. Phase 1 (ship vs ship) carries into phase 2: enemy hull ≤50% shakes their crew (-15%, or -30% at ≤25%), grapeshot and hull hits thin your crew, aimed shots injure officers, and a clean or battered approach gives Advantage or Disadvantage. In normal fights you can board at ≤50% hull (full loot, chance of a prize ship or officer) or keep firing to sink her (about 40% loot). Weather shows before the fight (fair, weather gauge yours or theirs, squall, calm, fog), and every intent is shown as text. Off-phase cards can be swapped once per turn.
• Officers: quartermaster, bosun, gunner and navigator, with 2 candidates each (8 total, using the wiki names lightly). Each has a signature card plus a passive. You get them at Recruit nodes, events and sometimes from boarding. They are injured, never killed (the card is lost until a tavern heals them).
• Cargo with a hold limit: powder fuels cannon cards (no powder = half damage), timber and rope repair at coves, food and water are used each leg (shortage hurts morale and crew; morale 0 = mutiny), rum gives +15 morale, silver sells at a Fence, and charts open wreck nodes.
• Ship ladder: periagua → sloop → brigantine → snow → frigate, each with a perk. A Shipwright sells hull/guns/rig upgrades (2 levels) and trades in your ship. Boarded enemies can be taken as prize ships.
• Three acts. Act I (10 legs) ends with the pirate-hunter squadron: sink the escort, then the frigate flagship, which you must board. Act II (short) ends with a Guarda Costa stockade crew assault. Act III (short) ends with Hornigold as a multi-stage boss: ship, then boarding, then a captain-vs-captain duel. Losing your ship at a boss sends you back one leg to retry once; a second loss means capture (run over).
• After each act boss: a trophy (Commodore's Pennant, a forged Letter of Marque, Hornigold's Spyglass) and the choice to take your plunder and sail home (75% banked) or press on. Payouts: death 25%, extraction 75%, final win 125%.
• Meta in localStorage: a Logbook (runs, wins, sinkings, boardings, best act, trophies, per-captain record), Banked Silver, and a Nassau base with 3 buildings at 2 levels (Shipyard, Powder House, Tavern) that give starting bonuses.
• Kept from v1.1: the ☰ rules legend (rewritten for v2), End Turn as a normal button at the bottom of the page, and a phone-first layout. The shop bug is fixed: the price wrapper is now the 31%-wide flex item, and the card fills it.
• Balance by simulation: a simple scripted bot (never extracts, takes the first card offered) wins about 19-28% of 120 runs, which works out to a first win in roughly 4-5 runs. Bosses were the walls (stockade crew, Hornigold's duel) and were tuned down. The bot does worst with Bellamy and Vane and best with Bonny & Read and Blackbeard.
• Rough or stubbed in v2: Acts II-III have small enemy pools and no unique events; there are 7 events; art is placeholder (one ship image for every enemy); the Nassau base has only 3 buildings; captains are not yet balanced against each other; there are no animations or sound.

# 4. Research Findings
## Slay the Spire (Mega Crit)
• Act structure: each act has 17 floors. Floor 1 is all normal fights, floor 9 is all Treasure (a guaranteed relic mid-act), floor 15 is all Rest Sites, floor 16 is the boss, then a boss chest. 3 main acts plus a short Act 4. Source: https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Map_Generation and https://teemo.dev/game-design/slay-the-spire/systems/map-path-and-act-structure/
• Map shape: a 7-column by 15-row grid with 6 paths drawn; every room has 1-3 exits. Source: https://www.reddit.com/r/slaythespire/comments/ndqweh/i_have_reverseengineered_map_generation_algorithm/
• Room weights for unfixed rooms: Normal fight 53%, Unknown (?) 22%, Rest 12%, Elite 8% (x1.6 at Ascension 1+), Merchant 5%. Source: https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Map_Generation
• Placement rules: no Elites or Rests in the first 5 floors; no Rest on floor 14; Elite/Rest/Merchant can't repeat back to back on a path; sibling rooms can't share a type. Source: Reddit reverse-engineering post above and https://slayspiredb.com/guides/slay-the-spire-map-generation-room-counts-room-odds-and-elites
• ? rooms: base odds Fight 10%, Merchant 3%, Treasure 2%, otherwise Event; a type that didn't roll grows by its base chance next time (pity system). Source: slayspiredb.com guide above
• Card rewards: pick 1 of 3 or skip. Rarity odds: normal fight 60% common / 37% uncommon / 3% rare; elite 50/40/10; boss 100% rare. A hidden rare-pity offset starts at -5% and rises 1% per common rolled (max +40%). Source: https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Card_Rewards
• Gold income: normal fight 10-20, elite 25-35, boss 95-105; chests 23-27 / 45-55 / 68-82. Source: https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Gold
• Shop prices: common card 45-55, uncommon 68-82, rare 135-165; one card always 50% off; relics 143-315 by rarity; potions 48-105. Card removal: 75 gold, +25 each use, once per shop. Source: https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Merchant
• Philosophy: 'every card should have its place' (and every relic, event, monster); balance comes from playtests plus a metrics server logging real runs; rare, hard-to-assemble overpowered combos are allowed because it's single-player. Source: Anthony Giovannetti, GDC 2019 'Slay the Spire: Metrics Driven Design and Balance' https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025731/-Slay-the-Spire-Metrics
• Why rewards feel good: always a choice (or skip); elites are optional, riskier fights that pay better (more gold, better card odds, a relic); a guaranteed treasure mid-act and a rest before the boss pace the tension.
## Balatro (LocalThunk)
• Run structure: 8 Antes x 3 Blinds (Small, Big, Boss). Small/Big can be skipped for a Tag (risk/reward: give up the shop and cash for a bonus); Boss Blinds can't be skipped and add a rule twist. Source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes
• Score curve: base requirement 300, 800, 2,000, 5,000, 11,000, 20,000, 35,000, 50,000 for Antes 1-8; Small = 1x, Big = 1.5x, Boss usually 2x. The target grows much faster than linear, so the player must build an engine, not just add cards. Source: same page
• Payouts: $3 Small, $4 Big, $5 Boss, $8 final boss, plus $1 per unused hand. Interest: $1 per $5 held, capped at $5 per round, which rewards saving up to $25. Source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Interest and https://balatrowiki.org/w/Gameplay_loop
• Shop after every Blind: reroll starts at $5 and rises $1 per reroll, resetting next shop. Source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Shop
• What's different: power comes from Jokers (persistent rule-bending modifiers, like relics) that multiply scoring; the money economy (interest, skip tags, rerolls) is itself a strategy layer; frequent shops give constant small choices.
## Dicey Dungeons (Terry Cavanagh)
• Run structure: 6 floors per episode. Floor 1 is tiny, floor 2 small, floors 3-4 normal, floor 5 big, floor 6 the boss. Each floor has 2-4 enemies, and you don't have to fight them all. Sources: https://wiki.diceydungeons.com/doku.php?id=maps and https://wiki.diceydungeons.com/doku.php?id=dicey_dungeons
• Economy: each kill gives XP equal to the enemy's level plus a coin, which pays for chests and shops; leveling up fully heals you and can add dice. Source: dicey_dungeons wiki page above
• Dice: roll 1-6 at the start of each turn (Warrior, Thief and Inventor start with 2 dice; Witch with 3). You place dice into equipment, and the die value usually sets the effect's strength. Source: https://wiki.diceydungeons.com/doku.php?id=mechanics%3Adice
• Equipment instead of a deck: 6 battle slots plus a 20-slot backpack; found in chests, bought in shops, swapped at trading posts; one free upgrade at the anvil. Source: https://wiki.diceydungeons.com/doku.php?id=equipment
• What's different: no draw luck, only dice luck; level-up full heals replace rest sites; dice manipulation (reroll, split, +1) is the skill expression; taking damage charges a Limit Break.
• Inscryption (Daniel Mullins): its sacrifice/blood/bones resource economy, lane combat and tug-of-war scale came out of a 48-hour Ludum Dare 43 jam; card balance was tuned through Steam Playtest; card effects use clear symbols instead of text for legibility. Sources: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-game-jam-sacrifices-became-inscryption and https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/-i-inscryption-s-i-journey-from-game-jam-joint-to-cult-classic
• Takeaway from Inscryption: atmosphere and story discoveries between fights (the cabin, talking cards) carry a lot of the fun. The equivalent for us is Nassau and crew events. Use icons to cut text on small screens.
# 5. Balance Targets for Free Nassau
• Act length (v1.1): 9 legs plus the boss, 3 columns wide. Our act is shorter than Slay the Spire's 15 floors, so fixed rows pace it: leg 1 is all fights, leg 5 is all Treasure, leg 9 is all Tavern (rest before the boss).
• Node weights for the other legs (v1.1): Fight 45%, Event 22%, Tavern 12%, Elite 10%, Merchant 8%, Treasure 3%. Compared with Slay the Spire, fights drop from 53% to 45% and Merchant rises from 5% to 8%, which answers 'too fight-heavy'.
• Placement (v1.1): no Elite or Tavern on legs 1-2; no Tavern on leg 8; Elite/Tavern/Merchant never twice in a row in the same column; a Merchant is guaranteed on legs 3 and 7 and an Elite on leg 6.
• Events (v1.1): 10% chance of an ambush fight. Later, add a pity system like Slay the Spire's.
• Expected run (target): about 5-6 normal fights, 1-2 elites, 2-3 events, 1-2 merchants, 1-2 treasures, 1-2 taverns, then the boss.
• Card reward odds (v1.1): normal fights 57% common / 37% uncommon / 6% rare, pick 1 of 3. Elites 45/40/15, pick 1 of 4. Shop 54/37/9. Target: add a rare-pity offset, plus a 'boss = all rare' reward when Act 2 exists.
• Coin income (v1.1): normal fights 15-25, elites 30-40 plus 2 cargo, Treasure 25-50 plus a relic, and cargo sells for 3-25 each. Start with 99 coin so the first Merchant is a real shopping trip.
• Shop prices (v1.1): common 45-55, uncommon 68-82, rare 135-165, one card 50% off; relic 120; removal 50 coin, +25 each time, once per visit. Target: about 1 good purchase per Merchant visit with ~60 coin earned between Merchants.
• Relic frequency (v1.1): start with 1, then guaranteed from every Elite and Treasure, or buy one at the Merchant. Target: 3-5 relics by the boss.
• Player HP and damage (v1.1): Ren has 60 HP. Normal enemies have 28-46 HP, scaling +5% per leg; enemies deal about 7-12 damage per turn; the boss has 110 HP and hits up to 22. Target: a normal fight costs 5-15 HP with good play, and an elite 15-25.
• Deck and energy: 3 energy, draw 5, an 11-card starter deck (Slay the Spire uses 3 energy, draw 5, 10 starter cards). Target: most decks end at 15-22 cards, and removals should feel valuable.
• Run length: about 15-25 minutes per run in Act 1. Full game target: 3 acts, about 45-60 minutes.
• To measure: log each run (floor of death, HP lost per fight, cards picked or skipped) to localStorage, in the spirit of Mega Crit's metrics server, and tune from real play.

## 9.1 Feedback on v1.1 (nothing decided; no code changed this round)
• Shop layout is broken: the card column gets very narrow and the text looks bad.
• Make the game more general: drop the specific story characters. Any named figures should be real, recognizable historical pirates. The current captain art can stay as a placeholder.
• Pull more from the reference games, especially Inscryption, plus Slay the Spire, Balatro and Dicey Dungeons.
• A touch too easy. Choices should matter more than luck.
• Cargo has no use besides selling. Give it a real purpose or fold it into plunder/silver. 'Sell at shop' is odd when you can also get cargo at the shop.
• Open to meta progression (permanent upgrades, a win record) and difficulty options (Ascension in Slay the Spire, Stakes/'Heat' in Balatro). Nothing decided yet.
• Shop bug, root cause (code read only, not fixed): each shop card is wrapped in a plain <div> (card + price label), and that wrapper is the flex item in .cards. .card has width:31%, so its width is 31% of the wrapper. The wrapper has no width of its own and sizes itself from its content, so the percentage resolves against a shrink-wrapped box and collapses to roughly the longest word. That is why only the shop is narrow; reward and hand cards sit directly in .cards. Possible fixes for later: give the wrapper the 31%/118px width and make the card 100%, or put the price inside the card.
## 9.2 Findings: Inscryption deep dive (Act I / Kaycee's Mod)
• Board and lanes: 4 lanes per side. At the bell, each creature hits the card directly opposite; if the lane is empty, the damage goes to the opponent. Excess damage can carry to a card behind. Positioning is the core decision. Source: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Cards
• Scales (not HP): damage tips a scale using teeth. Five points toward one side wins that round. A lead you build is spent defending, so a fight is a tug-of-war, not an HP race. Sources: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Cards and https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-game-jam-sacrifices-became-inscryption
• Candles = lives: losing a fight snuffs a candle; losing all candles ends the run. Kaycee's Mod has a 'Single Candle' challenge. Sources: https://primagames.com/gaming/inscryption-beginners-guide-card-upgrades-vendors/2 and https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Kaycee%27s_Mod
• Blood (sacrifice) costs: no mana. To play a 1-3 Blood card, you sacrifice creatures already on the board, each worth 1 Blood. Each turn you draw either from your deck or a free Squirrel from a separate 10-card side deck. Squirrels exist to be sacrificed or to block. Every card you play is a trade-off. Sources: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Squirrel and https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Cards
• Bones: a pooled second currency. You gain 1 Bone whenever one of your creatures dies, including by sacrifice, and spend Bones on Bone-cost cards. Losing creatures becomes fuel, so bad trades still pay something. Source: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Bones
• Sigils: icon-based keyword abilities on cards (e.g. Airborne, Mighty Leap, Touch of Death, Unkillable, Fecundity). They are transferable: sacrifice one card to move its sigil onto another, at the cost of losing a card. Icons are used instead of text so cards stay readable. Sources: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Totem and https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/-i-inscryption-s-i-journey-from-game-jam-joint-to-cult-classic
• Campfire: a push-your-luck upgrade. +1 Power or +2 Health per warming. Each extra warming risks the survivors eating the card: 22.5%, then 45%, then 67.5% in Act I (Kaycee's Mod: only 2 buffs, the second at 50% risk). The player chooses how far to push. Source: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Campfire
• Totems (Woodcarver): a head (a tribe) plus a body (a sigil) gives every card of that tribe the sigil, building around tribes over a run. Enemies get totems in 'Totem Battles'. Source: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Totem
• Other card nodes: pick 1 of 3; choose a cost and get a random card of it; choose a tribe; Card Swap; Build-a-Card. Currencies: Teeth (from overkill damage) buy Pelts, which you trade for cards. Sources: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Cards and https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Act_I
• Deathcards (permadeath becomes content): when a run ends, you build a Deathcard from 3 random cards in your deck (one supplies cost, one stats, one sigils). It can appear in later runs as a reward. Source: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Deathcard
• Leshy's narrative layer: Leshy is a game-master narrator across the table who describes every event, wears masks to play the bosses, and comments on your choices. The world is presented as a tabletop game being run for you. Mullins said much of the draw is 'outside of the rules': the antagonist, the cabin, talking cards. Sources: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Act_I and https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-game-jam-sacrifices-became-inscryption
• Kaycee's Mod (Ascension-style ladder): opt-in challenges worth Challenge Points (e.g. Tipped Scales 20 CP = start each fight 1 down; More Difficult 15 CP; Single Candle 30 CP; Grizzly Bosses 50 CP). Winning with enough CP unlocks starter decks and cards. It also has anti-reset-scumming: quitting in the first area weakens your next start. Source: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Kaycee%27s_Mod
## 9.3 Findings: skill over luck, difficulty ladders, and giving currencies a purpose
• Telegraphed information (Slay the Spire): enemy intents show the next action, so each turn is a puzzle you can solve, not a gamble. We already do this; it's the base of 'choices over luck'.
• Deck thinning and removal (Slay the Spire): removal costs 75 gold, +25 per use, once per shop. A smaller deck draws its best cards more often, which turns a luck problem into a planning problem. Source: https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Merchant
• Pity and smoothing systems (Slay the Spire): the rare-card offset climbs after every common, and '?' room odds rise for whatever hasn't appeared. Both cut down long streaks of bad luck. Sources: https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Card_Rewards and https://slayspiredb.com/guides/slay-the-spire-map-generation-room-counts-room-odds-and-elites
• Draw control (Inscryption): each turn you choose to draw from your deck or a free Squirrel from the side deck, so the player decides between tempo and power. Source: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Cards
• Dice you can manipulate (Dicey Dungeons): luck is the input, and equipment turns it into choices (you decide which die goes where; some gear rerolls, splits or bumps dice). Limit Break charges when you take damage (about a third of max HP), which softens bad streaks. Sources: https://wiki.diceydungeons.com/doku.php?id=mechanics%3Adice and https://wiki.diceydungeons.com/doku.php?id=mechanics%3Alimit_break
• Risk you choose (Inscryption campfire, Balatro skip tags, Slay the Spire elites): the best rewards sit behind risks the player opts into, so luck is something you accept on purpose.
• Scaling enemies: Balatro's score targets climb steeply (300 → 50,000 over 8 Antes), so you need an engine; Slay the Spire scales enemies by act. Easy-feeling runs usually mean enemy scaling is flatter than player power growth. Source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes
• Difficulty ladders:
• Slay the Spire Ascension 1-20 (cumulative; unlocked by winning the level below): A1 more elites (~60%); A2-4 deadlier normals/elites/bosses; A5 heal 75% after bosses; A6 start damaged; A10 a curse card; A14 lower max HP; A15 worse events; A16 shops +10%; A20 double boss. Source: https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Ascension
• Balatro Stakes (8, cumulative, per deck): Red = Small Blind pays nothing; Green/Purple = targets scale faster; Black = Eternal jokers; Blue = -1 discard; Orange = Perishable jokers; Gold = Rental jokers. Source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Stakes
• Inscryption Kaycee's Mod: pick-your-own challenges for Challenge Points; unlocks come from total CP. Source: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Kaycee%27s_Mod
• How each game gives currencies a purpose:
• Slay the Spire: one currency (gold) with clear sinks: cards, relics, potions, removal. Gold-for-HP and gold-for-relic trades happen in events. Source: https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Gold
• Balatro: money is both a spend and an investment. Interest ($1 per $5, capped at $5) rewards saving; rerolls ($5, +$1 each) are a sink; Jokers can be sold back. Sources: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Interest and https://balatrowiki.org/w/Shop
• Inscryption: every resource is tied to the board. Blood comes from sacrifice, Bones from deaths, Teeth from overkill, Pelts are bought with Teeth and traded for cards. Each currency comes from a different way of playing. Sources: https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Bones and https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Act_I
• Dicey Dungeons: coins from kills buy equipment, apples (healing) and upgrades at shops (typically 1-4 gold); upgrades are also free at Ned's anvil. Costs are tiny, so every coin is a decision. Sources: https://wiki.diceydungeons.com/doku.php?id=yolanda and https://wiki.diceydungeons.com/doku.php?id=ned
• Historical pirates who could be named (all real and tied to Nassau, about 1713-1718): Benjamin Hornigold, Henry Jennings, Edward Teach ('Blackbeard', voted Nassau's magistrate), Charles Vane, 'Calico Jack' Rackham, Anne Bonny, Mary Read, 'Black Sam' Bellamy, Stede Bonnet; antagonist: Governor Woodes Rogers (arrived July 1718 with the King's Pardon; Hornigold took it and hunted his old comrades; Vane escaped with a fireship). ## 9.4 Gaps in v1.1 and options (undecided)
• Gap: setting is tied to one story's characters. Options: (A) generic 'a free captain' with real pirates as rivals, bosses and allies (Hornigold, Vane, Blackbeard, Rackham, Bonny, Read, Bellamy); (B) pick a historical captain as the player character (start-deck choice like Kaycee's Mod decks); (C) an Inscryption-style narrator, e.g. an old quartermaster telling the tale at a tavern table.
• Gap: combat is 'Slay the Spire lite' with no unique hook. Options: (A) keep the energy/cards system and add a ship-vs-ship layer (hull vs. crew damage, broadside vs. boarding range); (B) Inscryption-style lanes: crew cards in 3-4 deck positions, sacrifice crew for 'blood' to play bigger cards, a Bones-like 'fallen crew' pool; (C) a dice pool you assign to cards (Dicey Dungeons), with cards as equipment slots.
• Gap: win and lose conditions are a plain HP race. Options: (A) keep HP; (B) Inscryption scales: a 'tide' or 'morale' tug-of-war where you win at +5; (C) two tracks, hull HP and crew morale, with either one hitting 0 losing the fight.
• Gap: cargo has no purpose, and selling at the shop clashes with buying there. Options: (A) fold all cargo into one 'plunder/silver' currency; (B) cargo as fuel: gunpowder powers cannon cards, rum heals or buys morale, timber/rope repair hull, food/water are paid per leg (upkeep); (C) cargo as Inscryption-style trade goods, like Pelts, only tradable at a fence or trading post for cards and relics, never sold at the merchant.
• Gap: luck outweighs choices (dice cards, random rewards) and it's too easy. Options: removal/thinning priced like Slay the Spire; rare pity; dice manipulation cards (reroll, +1, set); a free side-deck draw (Inscryption Squirrels, e.g. 'deckhands'); steeper enemy scaling and smarter multi-intent enemies; push-your-luck upgrades (campfire-style 'careen the ship').
• Gap: no reason to replay after a win. Options: (A) a win record and stats only; (B) unlocks: new starting captains, decks and cards (Kaycee's Mod, Balatro decks); (C) Nassau rebuilding (permanent buildings); (D) Deathcards: a lost run's captain becomes a card or ghost ship in later runs.
• Gap: no difficulty settings. Options: (A) a numbered ladder like Ascension ('Notoriety 1-10'); (B) Balatro-style Stakes per starting captain; (C) Kaycee's Mod a-la-carte challenges worth points ('Bounty').
• Gap: presentation is functional. Options: an Inscryption-style narrator voice and tabletop framing; icon sigils instead of rules text; real ship art per enemy; per-captain portraits.

# 11. v2 Design Outline (draft for review)
• 11.1 Pillars
• Captain, ship and crew: three linked layers. The captain is the heart; losing the ship or crew hurts but doesn't end the run.
• Choices over luck: chance comes from card draw, enemy loadouts, wind/weather and risky event choices. All of it is visible before you commit.
• Attrition is the challenge: damage carries over and nothing tops off, so winning efficiently matters as much as winning.
• Cargo has a job: every good is fuel, repair, upkeep, morale or a key. Silver/plunder is the only pure money.
• Light history: real Golden Age captains and events (1713-1718), worn lightly.
• Phone-first: readable cards, icons for keywords (Inscryption lesson), no floating UI.
• 11.2 Run structure
• 3 acts, each a 3-wide branching map (forward/diagonal moves, kept from v1) of about 10-12 legs ending in a boss.
• Act 1, The Flying Gang (Nassau waters): rival pirates, merchants, light navy patrols. Boss: a pirate-hunter frigate squadron (multi-ship fight).
• Act 2, The Pardon (Florida Straits / treasure-fleet waters): Spanish guarda costas, salvagers, privateers. Boss: a crew-fight boss (a fortified salvage camp stormed on foot) or a captain duel, drawn from a pool.
• Act 3, The Hunter (Windward Passage / Bahamas): Royal Navy and pardoned pirates turned hunters. Final boss: Benjamin Hornigold, in a 3-stage encounter: ship battle, then boarding, then a captain-alone duel.
• Fixed beats per act: leg 1 fight; a mid-act treasure/prize row; a Careening Cove (rest) row before the boss.
• Real events as choices (light): Woodes Rogers' arrival at Nassau (1718) as an Act 3 story beat, the 1715 treasure-fleet wreck salvage, Vane's fireship gambit, Blackbeard's Charleston blockade (ransom for medicine).
• 11.3 Captains (pick one per run; each has its own starter deck, passive and captain HP). Launch set (decided): Blackbeard, Bellamy, Vane, Bonny & Read. Rackham and Bonnet are later unlocks.
• Benjamin Hornigold is the final boss, so he isn't playable in v2.
• Edward 'Blackbeard' Teach, Terror: the starter deck leans on intimidation (enemy crew morale damage, Fear on boarders). Passive: boarding starts with the enemy crew -10% morale. Higher HP.
• 'Black Sam' Bellamy, Prince of Pirates: a crew-and-plunder deck. Passive: +1 silver per fight and recruits cost less; crew morale holds better. (Bellamy was known for treating crews fairly; flavor.)
• Charles Vane, Defiance: an aggressive ship and fire deck (fireship, chain shot). Passive: start with gunpowder +2; burning the enemy deals damage over time. Low morale tolerance.
• Anne Bonny & Mary Read (a duo as one captain): a hand-to-hand and duel deck. Passive: the captain gains a second action in boarding; strongest in captain-alone moments.
• Stede Bonnet, the Gentleman Pirate (unlock): an economy and learning deck. Passive: buys ships and upgrades cheaper but starts with weaker crew skill. A deliberately quirky 'hard mode' captain.
• 'Calico Jack' Rackham (unlock): a fast-sloop raider. Passive: +1 speed, so he picks wind position first; fragile hull.
• 11.4 Officers (one per role; each has a signature card plus a passive; injured, not killed)
• Slots: Quartermaster, Bosun, Master Gunner, Navigator (core 4), plus Surgeon and Boarding Master (unlocked by a bigger ship or Nassau buildings). 4-6 slots, which answers 'how many'. Roles must stay mechanically distinct (no overlapping jobs), and each role grows a pool of candidates over time (unlocks). Start of run (decided): only the captain, a basic ship and basic crew, with no officers. Officers are recruited through encounters (e.g. a beaten enemy officer, a rescued prisoner), payment (Recruit/Tavern nodes) or events. They are never granted automatically, except in rare story beats.
• Injury: an officer KO'd in a fight is Injured. Their passive is off and their card is weakened until treated (Surgeon, Careening Cove or coin). Two injuries in a row puts them on the beach until the next Recruit/Tavern node (they're not lost).
• Quartermaster (morale, plunder split, discipline). Candidates: Henry Jennings (real; plunder bonus), Mistress Alannah's factor (Ren-original setting, light; better sale prices), 'a hard-bargaining purser' (generic). Signature card: 'Fair Shares' (restore morale, spend silver). Passive: morale loss -25%.
• Bosun (hull, rigging, crew work). Candidates: Tom Pruitt and Barty Shea (Ren-original names, light), 'a grizzled bosun' (generic). Signature: 'All Hands' (repair hull using timber/rope; cheaper repair). Passive: +X hull repair at Careening Cove.
• Master Gunner (cannon phase). Candidates: Israel Hands (real; Blackbeard's master), 'a Royal Navy deserter' (generic), Abel Hatch (Ren-original name). Signature: 'Double-Shotted' (next broadside x2, costs 2 gunpowder). Passive: the first cannon card each fight is free.
• Navigator (map and wind). Candidates: 'a Spanish pilot' (generic), Matthias Penhallow (Ren-original name, light), 'a charts thief' (generic). Signature: 'Read the Wind' (shift the wind one step). Passive: see one extra leg of the map and one hidden ? result; food/water use -1 per 3 legs.
• Surgeon. Candidates: 'a barber-surgeon', 'a captured ship's doctor' (generic). Signature: 'Field Dressing' (heal captain or treat an officer). Passive: X% of fallen crew survive each fight.
• Boarding Master. Candidates: Owen Reardon or Nick Brand (Ren-original names), 'a Maroon fighter' (generic). Signature: 'Grapples Away' (start boarding with +1 advantage). Passive: boarding crew strength +10%.
• Officers level up (2 tiers) through rewards: an upgraded signature card and a stronger passive.
• 11.5 Crew (a number, not cards)
• Crew X/Max (e.g. 62/70). Max comes from the ship. Crew count scales boarding strength and some card effects ('per 10 crew').
• Morale 0-100: low morale weakens crew; at 0, a mutiny event (pay rum/silver, lose an officer, or fight your own crew). Rum, plunder shares and victories raise it; food/water shortages and losses lower it.
• Crew die in boarding and from heavy hull hits. Replenish at Recruit nodes, ports and taverns (costs silver, plus rum for better hands) or by pressing prisoners after a win (cheap, but lowers morale).
• Crew abilities (upgradable drills): Gun Drill (cannon cards), Boarding Drill, Sail Drill (speed). Bought at Nassau and Recruit nodes.
• 11.6 Ships (simple ladder; upgrades raise trade-in value)
• Ladder: Periagua (start option) → Sloop → Brigantine → Snow/Brig → Frigate (Act 3 prize). Bigger means more hull, guns (cannon-card cap), crew max and officer slots; smaller means faster.
• Minor perks: Sloop (fast: wind advantage, can flee some fights); Brigantine (balanced; +1 cargo hold); Snow (heavy broadsides); Frigate (most guns and crew, slow).
• Upgrades (3 per ship, 2 levels each): Hull (sheathing), Guns (more or heavier cannon), Rigging (speed/wind). Each upgrade adds about 50% of its cost to resale value.
• Swapping: take a captured prize after a ship battle (if the enemy struck rather than sank), or buy at Shipwright nodes. Trade-in value = base x condition (hull %) + 50% of upgrades.
• 11.7 Encounters
• Ship battle, phase 1 (ship vs ship): play a hand of cannon/sail/defense cards against a ship with a randomized loadout and readable intents. Wind/weather is shown before the fight (e.g. 'Weather gauge: you', 'Squall: -1 cannon card', 'Calm: no sail cards').
• Phase 1 outcome becomes boarding advantage: you choose when to board. The enemy's hull %, crew lost and your position become Advantage tokens (e.g. +2 cards drawn, an enemy crew penalty). Sink or board (decided, normal/random battles only; bosses are scripted): once the enemy hull is low you choose. Sink = fight ends safely (no boarding risk to crew, officers or captain), but loot is about 40% and there's no prize ship or documents. Board = full loot, a chance at the prize ship and enemy officers to recruit, at the risk of crew, officer and captain damage. Sinking is the smart call when you're hurt.
• Phase 2, boarding (crew hand-to-hand): the captain plus officers' cards versus enemy officers; crew numbers add a bonus to every turn. Win: take plunder and maybe the prize ship. Lose: break off and retreat with damage.
• Crew-only fights: shore raids, tavern brawls, mutinies.
• Captain-alone moments (rare): duels, an ambush ashore, escapes. Only captain cards, with high stakes and high reward (a relic/trophy).
• Bosses: Act 1 multi-ship (2-3 enemy ships with shared intents); Act 2 crew-fight or duel (drawn from a pool); Act 3 Hornigold in 3 stages (ship → boarding → duel).
• Shared combat system (decided): one deck, one 3-energy hand, the same turn rules in both phases. Cards are tagged Ship (phase 1 only), Crew (phase 2 only) or Captain (both); unplayable cards in a phase can be discarded to draw 1 (no dead hands). Phase 1 → phase 2 carryover (proposed specifics): (a) Enemy hull % at boarding sets enemy crew loss: <=25% hull = enemy crew -30% and their officers start Injured; <=50% = enemy crew -15%. (b) Grapeshot/anti-crew cards thin enemy numbers directly; enemy anti-crew shots thin yours. (c) Your hull damage taken in phase 1 kills crew (about 1 crew per 4 hull damage) and can Injure an officer on a critical hit (shown as a warning intent). (d) Advantage tokens (max 3) earned by phase-1 feats (out-sail the wind, rake the stern, dismast): each gives +1 card draw or +1 energy on boarding turn 1, or a morale buff; enemy feats give you Disadvantage (-1 draw, crew morale debuff). (e) Ship damage always carries over after the fight.
• 11.8 Health tracks and persistence
• Three tracks, all persistent between fights: Hull (ship), Crew (count plus morale), Captain HP.
• The run ends only when Captain HP hits 0. Hull at 0 (decided: severe but recoverable): a forced 'Abandon Ship' retreat event. You lose the ship and all its upgrades, about half your cargo and silver, and 30-50% of your crew; one officer may be Injured or left behind. You continue in a basic periagua or sloop (keeping your deck) and must rebuild through Shipwright nodes and prizes. Crew at 0: you can't board until you recruit.
• No auto-heal after fights. Healing comes from Careening Cove (hull, using timber/rope), Surgeon/Tavern (captain), Recruit (crew), and specific cards/events.
• 11.9 Cargo (each good has one clear job)
• Gunpowder: fuels cannon cards (heavy shots cost 1-2). Running out means only light guns.
• Timber and Rope: repair hull at Careening Cove, or mid-run with Bosun cards.
• Food and Fresh Water: 1 of each per leg. Shortage: -morale, then crew loss.
• Rum: raises morale, recruits better hands, and calms a mutiny.
• Silver/Plunder: money, mostly from fights. Sell at Fences/ports (not the Merchant), or bank it in Nassau between runs.
• Maps and Documents: unlock hidden nodes/events (wreck sites, treasure caches, an elite with a guaranteed relic, pardon intrigue).
• Hold capacity comes from the ship (e.g. Sloop 12, Frigate 30), so taking plunder versus supplies is a real choice.
• To avoid overload: only 6 cargo icons on the HUD, upkeep shown before each move, auto-buy suggestions at ports.
• 11.10 Map and nodes
• Nodes: Fight (ship battle), Elite (navy/notable pirate with a guaranteed trophy), Crew Fight, ? Event (risky choices, real events), Port/Merchant (buy cards and supplies), Fence (sell plunder and documents), Shipwright (upgrade, buy, trade in), Recruit (hire or swap an officer of a shown role, add crew), Tavern (captain heal, rumors, optional dice gambling minigame as flavor), Careening Cove (hull repair; rest), Treasure/Wreck, Boss.
• Recruit nodes show which officer role they offer on the map, so you can route for a Master Gunner, for example.
• Hidden nodes are revealed by Maps/Documents.
• Weights (starting point, from Slay the Spire research): Fight 40%, Event 20%, Port/Fence 10%, Recruit 8%, Elite 8%, Careening Cove 8%, Shipwright 4%, Treasure 2%, plus fixed rows.
• 11.11 Rewards (three competing upgrade paths)
• After each win, choose ONE of: a Ship upgrade/part, a Crew/Officer upgrade (drill, officer tier, recruit), or a Captain card/upgrade. Plus plunder and silver.
• Elites and bosses offer 2 picks or a Trophy (relic-like, e.g. 'Hornigold's Commission').
• Card upgrades are available at Ports (pay) and Careening Cove (instead of repair: the 'rest or upgrade' choice from Slay the Spire).
• Rarity and pity, card removal at Ports (Slay the Spire style; rising cost, once per visit).
• 11.12 Difficulty
• Base philosophy: the first win should take about 3-6 runs for a deck-builder fan. Difficulty comes from attrition across fights, not single-fight spikes; every loss should trace to visible choices (routes, cargo, risky events).
• Fairness rules: enemy intents and loadouts are always visible; wind is shown before engaging; risky events state their odds or show a 'risk' meter (Inscryption campfire).
• Notoriety ladder (unlocks after the first win; each level adds to the previous, like Slay the Spire Ascension and Balatro Stakes):
• N1: more Elites on the map. N2: enemy ships get +1 gun. N3: food/water upkeep +50%. N4: start with 10% captain damage. N5: Careening Cove repairs 25% less. N6: officers start Injured more easily (one KO = Injured, two = gone for the act). N7: enemy crews +15%. N8: Ports cost +15%. N9: worse event outcomes. N10: Hornigold gets a 4th stage (his fleet joins) and a harder duel.
• Optional challenge toggles (Kaycee's Mod style) for bonus Infamy: Single Ship (no swaps), Dry Hold (no rum), Iron Captain (half captain HP), Pressed Men (start with 60% crew).
• 11.13 Meta progression and the Nassau base
• Record and stats: wins/losses by captain and Notoriety, best run, plunder banked, ships sunk, favorite cards; a 'Logbook' panel.
• Unlocks (Balatro-style milestones): win with any captain → Rackham; reach Act 3 → Bonnet; win at N3 → Frigate start option; specific feats unlock officers (e.g. win without losing an officer → Israel Hands).
• Carry-over currency: Banked Silver. 'Take your plunder and sail home' (decided) is an extraction choice offered ONLY at specific extraction points (after each act boss), never at any time. Proposed payouts: death keeps 25% of carried silver as Banked Silver and no trophies; extracting keeps 75% plus the meta rewards earned so far; a full win keeps 100% plus a +25% victory bonus. Boss trophies (Hades-style, decided): each act boss drops a rare unique item (e.g. the frigate captain's commission, Hornigold's spyglass). It's used in the run, kept permanently if you extract or win, and lost on death; trophies unlock Nassau upgrades or cosmetics.
• Nassau buildings (each 3 levels, roughly 100/250/500 banked silver): Shipyard (start ship +1 upgrade, unlocks Brigantine start), Powder House (+2 starting gunpowder), Tavern (an extra officer candidate at Recruit nodes; better rum prices), Market (+1 card choice at Ports), Chapel/Infirmary (officer injuries heal faster), Fort (unlocks Notoriety ladder levels and the challenge toggles), Chart House (start each act with 1 Map).
• Limiting power creep: buildings grant options and small starts (about 5-10%), not raw stats; the total meta bonus is capped; Notoriety levels raise difficulty faster than buildings give power; a 'Pure' toggle disables Nassau boosts for leaderboard-style runs.
• Mistress Alannah (Ren-original, light) can run the Nassau Market/Exchange as a non-emotional NPC.

# 12. Visual Direction (v2.1)
## 12.1 Research findings
• Slay the Spire, intents: MegaCrit replaced a text "next move" bar with an always-visible icon above each enemy. Sword icons get scarier as the damage grows, with shields for block and swirls for debuffs. Casey Yano: "usability improved so much that I don't think we have any regrets." Exact numbers were added later. (Ars Technica War Stories: arstechnica.com/video/watch/war-stories-slay-the-spire-war-stories; Rock Paper Shotgun: rockpapershotgun.com/why-revealing-all-is-the-secret-of-slay-the-spires-success)
• Slay the Spire, cards: card type is shown by border shape and colour family first, then the cost orb, then the text, so you can read a fanned hand without reading words. (teemo.dev/game-design/slay-the-spire/systems/art-direction-and-visual-language)
• Legibility: Zach Gage's "Designing for Subway Legibility" argues the most important info (the intents) should be the second read, not the third, and warns against everything being tiny icons at one scale. Takeaway: make the intent big, and keep the HUD small and secondary. (stfj.net/DesigningForSubwayLegibility)
• Inscryption (GDC 2022 postmortem, Daniel Mullins): cards use metaphor symbols instead of text, with a rulebook in the world for detail. The UI is diegetic: teeth on a scale show damage, candles show lives. (gamedeveloper.com/marketing/-i-inscryption-s-i-journey-from-game-jam-joint-to-cult-classic)
• Balatro, juice: input is instant and untimed, and the whole feel budget goes into output. Each card shows its cause and then its effect, with number pops and screen shake scaled to how big the moment is. It also offers animation-speed and reduced-motion settings, because juice becomes a tax by run 400. (teemo.dev/game-design/balatro/systems/game-feel-and-juice)
• "Juice It or Lose It" (Jonasson & Purho, GDC Europe 2012): tweening, particles and screen shake on impact, with the intensity kept modest. (gdcvault.com/play/1016487/juice-it-or-lose)
• Monster Train mobile port (Secret 6): PC hover tooltips became tap for key info and tap-and-hold for full detail, and screens were made more concise instead of just enlarged. (secret6.com/post/driving-monster-train-to-mobile)
• Dicey Dungeons: bright, expressive and readable on purpose (not grimdark). Each corner holds one core element: turn, player stats, enemy stats, End Turn. (gamedeveloper.com Road to the IGF; distractionware.com art director post; mechanicsofmagic.com visual design)
• Hades (Jen Zee): the art direction is led by game design, readable silhouettes and icon-first boons. Criticism: small HUD text and no text-size option. (mcvuk.com behind-the-art-of-hades)
• Phone layout: put frequent actions in the lower part of the screen, with touch targets of at least 44pt (iOS) or 48dp (Android). Respect safe-area insets. Steven Hoober's touch research says people tap and look at the centre most, and accuracy is worst in the corners, so keep key content central and leave space at the edges. (Apple HIG Game controls; developer.apple.com/videos/play/meet-with-apple/243; developer.playcanvas.com safe-area; 4ourthmobile.com/publications/designing-for-touch)
• Nautical art direction: Sea of Thieves uses stylized, "wonky", brush-marked shapes, saturated sea and sky, and weathered patched materials. Return of the Obra Dinn uses a 1-bit dithered engraving look. Both are strong, cheap-to-be-consistent pirate looks at opposite ends. (seaofthieves.com art-of-adventure; blog.playstation.com Lucas Pope on Obra Dinn's art style)
## 12.2 What v2.1 implemented (commit b54a984, no rule or balance changes)
• Fit to the screen: the app is a full-height column with a compact HUD at the top, the content in the middle and the actions bar (End Turn) at the bottom of the page. Combat and the map fit a 390×844 phone with no scrolling (checked by the test). It respects safe areas.
• HUD as gauges: captain HP, hull, crew and morale are coloured bars with the number inside. Cargo is icon+number chips (low food, water and powder turn red). Officers are 4 role medallions (lit, injured, or empty).
• Icon-first cards: cost is a brass coin and the tag is a corner icon (⛵ Ship, ⚔️ Crew, 👑 Captain). Powder cost is shown as 💥 pips and Exhaust as ✖. Card text is turned into icons (🎯 damage with your guns already added, 🌀 Evasion, 🛡️ Guard, ☠️👥 kill crew, 😵 Weak, 🔥 Burn, 🃏 draw, ⭐ Advantage). The full text is a tooltip (hover on desktop, press and hold on phones).
• Intents: a large icon plus a number that grows in size and colour by damage tier (Slay the Spire style), with the target shown (⛵ hull, 👥 crew, ❤️ captain) and the enemy's whole pattern as a small strip with the next move highlighted. Tap for the full text.
• Style: IM Fell English body text with Pirata One headings (Google Fonts), parchment with grain, a plank background, rope-border frames, brass buttons and coins.
• Juice: cards deal in at the start of each turn, played cards fly to the enemy, damage and block numbers pop up, the enemy flashes when hit, the screen shakes when you are hit (stronger for big hits), and ships bob. Honours prefers-reduced-motion.
• Ships: SVG ships with no generated art: mast count, hull colour, stripe, flag (merchant red ensign, navy, Spanish, Jolly Roger) and battle damage (torn sails, smoke, fire) vary by enemy. There is a stockade SVG for the Act II boss, crossed swords for the duel, and your own ship (by type) in the corner.
• Sea-chart map: the parchment chart has dashed ink routes between linked ports, your sailed route in red, a compass rose and rhumb lines, wax-seal style nodes, a glowing beacon on reachable ones, and a ⛵ marker on your position.

The game sits on the captain's desk: cards are playing cards, the HUD is a logbook, HP is a candle or hourglass, cargo is barrels on a shelf and the map is a real chart with a brass divider. Most unique, but slowest to read and the most work.
• Orientation: portrait first (current) suits one hand. Landscape suits the desk/diegetic option and tablets or desktop.
• Juice settings: add Balatro-style animation speed (1×/2×/4×) and a screen-shake toggle once there is more juice.
## 12.4 Art Style Guide (decided with Ren: storybook painterly with engraved sea-chart touches, captain's desk frame, phone portrait)
• Palette: warm parchment (#F0DFB8, #E4CC95), sepia ink (#2E1F10, #6B4A1F), walnut wood (#4A2E17), aged brass (#C9A24A), and Caribbean sea teal-to-deep-blue (#5F8FA0 → #1F4A5E). Accents only: oxblood red (#8E2418) for danger and the Navy ensign, and Spanish red-gold for Guarda Costa. No neon and no pure black or white. Shadows are warm brown, highlights warm cream.
• Lighting: golden-hour or lantern light from the upper left. Soft, warm key light with cool sea-blue fill. Gentle rim light to separate the silhouette. Mood ranges from sunny adventure (ports, events) to stormy dusk (bosses), but stays storybook, never grimdark.
• Brushwork: storybook gouache and oil, with visible but controlled strokes, soft edges in the background and crisp edges on the focal subject. Light paper texture. Engraved touches: fine sepia line hatching on sea, sky and borders, like an 18th-century chart, used as an accent and never covering the painting. Readable silhouettes first: the subject must read at 90 px wide.
• Subjects: historically grounded Golden Age (1715-1720) Caribbean. Ships are period-correct (sloops, brigantines, snows, frigates with accurate rigging and flags). Captains are recognizable historical figures in period dress. Nassau, when shown, is a large established port with a busy harbor full of ships, warehouses and a fort, not a shanty camp. No Ren or James likenesses.
• Framing: one clear focal subject, centred or on the rule of thirds, with a quiet edge (top and bottom 10%) so UI and text overlays fit. No text, logos, frames or borders baked into the image (the frames are CSS). Enemy ships are shown three-quarter broadside on calm-to-choppy water, fully in frame.
• Aspect ratios (portrait game): enemy ships 4:3 (shown about 300×225), captain and officer portraits 1:1 (cropped to a circle, face in the centre 60%), event scenes 3:2 (banner, about 390×260), backgrounds (desk, chart) 9:16 or 9:19.5 (1080×1920 or more), card art (later) 4:3 thumbnails.
• Desk frame: the HUD is a leather logbook strip, the map is a real chart pinned on the desk with a brass divider and compass, and the backgrounds are walnut planks, candle glow and scattered coins. The centre of the desk stays empty and low-contrast for gameplay.
• Reusable prompt template: "Storybook painterly illustration, gouache and oil with visible soft brushstrokes, subtle 18th-century engraved sea-chart line hatching in the sky and sea, warm golden-hour lighting from the upper left, palette of parchment cream, sepia, walnut brown, aged brass and Caribbean teal, oxblood red accents. Subject: [SUBJECT, period-accurate 1715-1720 Golden Age of Piracy]. Composition: [FRAMING], single clear focal subject, readable silhouette, quiet margins at top and bottom. Aspect ratio [RATIO]. No text, no letters, no logos, no border, no frame, not photorealistic, not 3D render, not anime, not grimdark."
• Consistency rules: always use the template unchanged except for the brackets. Keep a reference sheet of approved images and pass 1-2 of them as style references when the tool allows it. Reject images with baked-in text, wrong-era clothing or rigging, extra limbs or modern items. Name files as type-subject-v#.png.
# 13. v3 Simplification and Playtest Notes
Ren's feedback was that the game was still too busy. v3 removes crew morale, merges food and water into Rations, and then audits every resource, stat, keyword, node and screen element against four questions: does it change a decision at least once per run, is it a distinct verb, can it be read on a phone without a tooltip, and does the sim show it matters. Live build: https://whyhatt.github.io/free-nassau/. Full notes are in the repo under notes/.
## 13.1 What was cut or merged, and why
Resources went from 11 things to 4: Silver (Coin and the Silver cargo merged; one currency that buys everything and is also your banked score), Gunpowder, Rations (food + water), and Repair kits (timber + rope). Rum, charts and the hold limit are gone; each supply now has its own small cap (14 / 12 / 6).
Morale is removed everywhere it appeared: the mutiny screen, rum, the fence bonus, victory and retreat swings, the low-morale boarding penalty, the HUD gauge, two events and the Tavern building. Its roles were rebalanced: Rally the Crew, Prince's Share and Fair Shares now restore crew (3 / 6 / 5) which is the number that actually dies; no rations costs 4 crew per leg; retreating from boarding costs 20% of the crew; Jennings gives +15% silver; the Tavern building gives crew and rations.
Keywords went from 8 to 5: Evasion, Guard and Block all meant the same thing and are now Block; Strength became plain text on one card; Weak, Burn, Advantage and Exhaust remain. The enemy Double-load intent (a hidden stacking buff) was removed. Weather lost Calm and Fog.
Nodes went from 10 to 8: Fence, Tavern (with the dice game) and Wreck are cut. Healing moved to a Surgeon in ports and a choose-one at the cove (careen the ship OR rest ashore); ports also gained a Carpenter for hull repairs. The ship reward is now a deterministic next upgrade. HUD: 3 gauges, coin, 3 supply icons, officer badges. Combat: no pattern strip, no own-ship picture.
Added back where content was thin: Wrecker Cutter, Treasure Galleon, Navy Revenue Cutter, two new elites (Guarda Costa Capitana in Act II, Royal Navy Warship in Act III), and 12 events tagged by act (was 7). Notoriety N1 to N5 is implemented in light form and unlocked by winning.
## 13.2 What the playtests found
Method: 8 UI-driven runs (tapping the real phone UI at 390x844, five captains, Notoriety 0 to 5) plus about 2,800 headless bot runs.
Removing morale alone made the game trivial (84 to 95% bot wins). The old 25% came mostly from morale and starvation spirals; normal fights cost almost no hull or HP and the bot reached every boss near full health. Enemy damage now runs about 1.9x effective, flat across acts, with separate per-act boss dials.
Act III damage spiked once the act scale stacked on the new dial (28-damage broadsides against a 45-hull sloop, 3.7 ship losses per run, a death spiral after abandoning ship). Damage is now flat across acts, the escape periagua keeps full hull, and ports have a Carpenter. Ship losses per run: 3.7 to 1.2.
Dominated or dead cards: Parry beat Tack Hard and Hold the Rail outright (removed from the reward pool, still Bonny & Read's starter); Command was worse than Ship's Articles (cut); Boarding Axes equalled two Cutlasses (13 to 16 kills); Take the Weather Gauge 3 to 5 Block; Defiance now also gives 5 Block. Raking Fire, Crowd Sail and Fireship are still on the weak side, and about twelve cards are variations of "gain N Block".
Captains: Bellamy (10%) now starts with Jennings and 66 HP; Vane (17%) died at the Act II Stockade for lack of crew cards and gets Pistol Volley plus a better Defiance; Blackbeard swaps Hold the Rail for a second Swivel and has 78 HP; Rackham gets Pistol Volley and 68 HP. No officer is a must-pick or a dead pick (27 to 39% win rate with each).
Playtests also found three layout overflows (Board row plus a two-line log, the duel portrait, a hint line); all fixed, and the test now checks 12 screens for no scrolling. Select-then-confirm costs about 1,000 taps per run, and bosses (up to 17 to 22 turns for Hornigold) are the tedious part.
## 13.3 New numbers (bot win rate at Notoriety 0)
Before (v2.5, 240 runs) to after (v3, 400 runs): Blackbeard 33% to 24%; Bellamy 10% to 33%; Vane 17% to 32%; Bonny & Read 40% to 34%; all four 25% to 31%. Rackham (unlock) 21%. Target was 20 to 35% with balanced captains.
Where runs end (share of all runs, v2.5 to v3): Act I boss 8% to 4%, Act II 6% to 6%, Act III 61% to 60%. Notoriety: N1 33%, N3 16%, N5 3%. Ship losses per run 1.2; 13.7 fights per run.
The bot is a modest player (it never buys cards and picks rewards at random), so these are a comparative yardstick for balance, not a prediction of human win rates.

# 14. Player Critique (Gamer's-Eye Review)
A gamer's-eye review, written as a player who wants a finished product rather than a prototype. Each point is a bullet with the one-line fix that would address it. Nothing in this section has been built yet; no game code changed.
• Looks like a web page, not a game. Vector ships, tray and props sit next to painted art and read as prototype, and very little moves except cards. What would fix it: replace the vector props with painted layers and add ambient motion (sea, flags, smoke) so every screen feels alive.
• Battles don't show enough. The screen shows numbers and bars, with no big cannon volley or boarding charge, and hits don't feel powerful. What would fix it: add full-screen volley, impact and boarding-charge sequences with screen shake, hit-stop and big damage numbers.
• Art repeats. Several enemies share one painting, and every card uses the same small etched icon style; card art is the biggest gap against Slay the Spire and Inscryption. What would fix it: commission unique illustrations for every enemy and card, starting with the 20 most-played cards.
• Thin content. There are few captains and a limited set of cards and events, and about 60% of runs end at the same final boss, so replay value needs variety. What would fix it: grow the pool (more captains, cards and events, plus a second Act III boss) so each run feels different.
• Cards feel small on a phone. Reading a card means tapping it and looking at a separate panel, and big hands collapse into a grid. What would fix it: print the key number on the card face and enlarge the touched card in a fanned hand.
• Runs are long and tap-heavy. Select-then-confirm is safe but slow. What would fix it: a speed-up toggle, skippable animations, one-tap play for experienced players and shorter boss fights.
• Sound and music. There is no music, only synthesized beeps and thuds. What would fix it: a looping shanty-style score for each act plus recorded cannon, rigging and sea effects.
• No tutorial or onboarding. Ship phase versus boarding, officers and sailing home all have to be discovered by trial and error. What would fix it: a short guided first voyage that teaches each of them in turn.
• Progress feels invisible. Notoriety, unlocks and the Nassau base are not presented as exciting rewards. What would fix it: a post-run reveal that shows Nassau growing, celebrates each unlock and keeps a visible progress track.
Top 3 highest-payoff fixes: (1) combat feedback and animation, (2) unique art for cards and enemies, (3) a short tutorial.
## Round 2: Harder Criticisms
The first nine points are what a player sees. These twelve are harder: I re-read the code and the notes, played the live build in a headless 390x844 browser, and ran 240 bot voyages on the current build (60 per base captain, Notoriety 0, 29% wins) plus two variant sims (never take a card reward; always take the middle lane). The bot is a modest player (it takes the first card offered and picks events at random), so treat the numbers as directional evidence about the design, not proof about human play. Estimates are marked. What held up: the title screen loads in about 2.8 seconds with 407 KB, a reload in the middle of combat resumes the same hand, safe-area insets are used and a Reduce motion toggle exists. Severity is High, Medium or Low. Each point gives the evidence, then a suggested fix.
• R2-1 [High] Acts I and II are a toll road; the run is decided at Hornigold. Evidence: only 22 of 240 runs (9%) died before Act III (6 in Act I, 16 in Act II), while 208 (87%) reached Hornigold with a median 81% captain HP and 79% hull and 149 runs (62%) died in Act III, 139 of them to Hornigold (68 in the duel, 61 in the ship phase, 10 while boarding). The state at the Act I boss does not predict the result (28% wins above median HP, 30% below), so early choices barely matter; the state at Hornigold does (47% above median HP, 19% below). A player feels ten safe fights and then a wall. Suggested fix: give Acts I and II real stakes (an elite that costs resources, ration pressure, a pursuit clock), and flatten the cliff by shortening the Hornigold duel and adding a checkpoint between his ship phase and the duel.
• R2-2 [High] Deck building is a weak decision; the ship tier is the real lever. Evidence: a bot that skips every card reward still wins 23% (54 of 240) against 29% (69 of 240) when it takes the first card offered, so the whole card economy is worth about 6 points. The final ship tier tracks results far more: periagua 11% (10 of 92), sloop 31% (24 of 78), brigantine 45% (17 of 38), snow 56% (18 of 32). That is a correlation, but it points to "buy the next hull" as the solved strategy. 14 of 32 cards involve Block and 22 of 32 cost 1 energy, so a card pick rarely changes a turn. Suggested fix: make cards build-arounds (powder, burn, boarding and officer synergies), add card removal or upgrade at ports, and give ship upgrades a trade-off (crew upkeep, slower sailing) instead of strictly better.
• R2-3 [Medium] Phase-locked cards create dead draws. Evidence: in the ship phase every Crew card is unplayable and in boarding every Ship card is. In the opening Blackbeard hand I captured, 3 of 6 cards were grey. Blackbeard starts with 6 Ship, 3 Crew and 1 Captain cards (Bellamy and Vane 5, 4, 1). A fight that ends by sinking never uses a Crew card at all, so a crew-heavy reward can be a dead pick, and the single swap per turn is the only relief. Suggested fix: let a locked card do a smaller version of its job in the wrong phase (Cutlasses give 3 Block at sea, a Swivel Gun covers boarding fire), or draw only playable cards on the first hand.
• R2-4 [Medium] Enemies are scripted loops with only the next move visible: memorizable, not plannable. Evidence: each ship enemy cycles a fixed list and ignores what you do (the Rival Pirate Sloop plays Broadside, Grapeshot, Broadside, Brace, Aimed Shot, then repeats, with base damage 7, 6, 5, 5, 5 scaled up per act; the Hornigold duel is Slash, Slash, Guard, Lunge). The v3 pass removed the pattern strip, so you see only the next intent: a veteran has the loops memorized while a newcomer cannot plan two turns ahead. Inside a fight the only randomness is the draw and a weather modifier of plus or minus 2 on a 10-point attack, which is noise rather than a decision. The luck that matters sits in reward offers and draws. Suggested fix: show the next two intents, give each enemy one reactive rule (it braces when your Block is up, boards when your crew is low), and randomize where each loop starts.
• R2-5 [Medium] The map is a three-lane ladder with shallow route choices. Evidence: each act is 3 columns by 10, 7 and 7 rows, you can only step one column sideways, and most nodes are the same mix (weights: fight 42, event 20, recruit 10, port 9, elite 8, shipwright 4; the first row is always a fight, the middle row a treasure, the last a cove). That is about 27 map screens per run with 2 or 3 buttons each. A bot that ignores the map and always takes the middle lane wins 24% (58 of 240) against 29% with simple route rules, and its Act I deaths jump from 6 to 29, so routing matters in Act I (recruit, port, cove) and almost nowhere else. Suggested fix: widen to 4 or 5 lanes with real forks, put elites and treasure on visible risk-reward branches, and let the player skip a leg by burning rations.
• R2-6 [High] Tap economy: hundreds of taps for a few dozen decisions. Evidence: ten UI-driven playtests in the notes (v3-era build) used 374 to 1,330 taps per run (median about 820, 5 to 15 fights), logging only 13 to 29 decisions each outside combat. Select-then-confirm makes every card play two taps, bot runs average 13.6 fights and about 550 engine steps, and the Hornigold fight alone has run 17 to 22 turns. A full run is probably 30 minutes or more on a phone (estimate, not timed). Suggested fix: one-tap play with an undo (or hold to read), auto end turn when nothing is playable, a 2x speed toggle, and cut Hornigold into two shorter stages.
• R2-7 [Medium] Meta progression is a slow, flat grind with little to unlock. Evidence: Nassau has 3 buildings with 2 levels each, costing 120 and 300 (Powder House), 120 and 300 (Tavern), 150 and 400 (Shipyard), 1,390 silver in total, and each only adds a flat starting bonus (+3 or +6 powder, +6 or +12 crew with rations, +1 Sheathing and Guns). Bot runs bank about 133 silver on average (about 389 per win, about 32 per Act III death, since death banks only 25%), so maxing the base takes about 10 runs, roughly 5 hours at the tap counts above (estimate). The only content unlocks are the Rackham captain and the Notoriety levels; nothing unlocks cards, events or ships. Suggested fix: unlock cards, officers, events and ships through milestones, give a death a guaranteed floor of silver per act reached, and make buildings change options rather than only add stats.
• R2-8 [Medium] The pirate fantasy lives in the menus, not in the turn. Evidence: a combat turn is energy, Block cards and attacks, which is the Slay the Spire template (14 of 32 cards involve Block). The pirate ideas (supplies, officers, extraction, prize ships) sit on the map and menu screens. The captain acts through a passive and two or three cards; there is no command verb such as giving orders, flying a flag, offering terms or splitting plunder, and boarding is a second round of the same cards. Suggested fix: add one captain-only action per turn (Orders: Fire at will, Hard to port, Parley), let reputation change enemy behaviour (a feared merchant strikes her colours), and move prize and plunder choices into combat.
• R2-9 [Medium] Accessibility gaps: zoom locked, tiny labels, small targets, one-handed reach. Evidence: the viewport meta sets maximum-scale=1 and user-scalable=no, which blocks pinch zoom, and there is no text-size setting. The headless audit of the combat screen at 390x844 found 7 text elements at 10.5 to 11px (Draw and Discard labels, the "/78" maxima), four 20x20 tap targets in the top HUD, a 23px-tall weather chip and a 34px enemy bar, all under the usual 44px target. The menu button is top right, the hardest spot for a thumb, while End Turn sits bottom centre (good). Red and blue ink arcs and bars carry meaning but numbers and icons back them up; I have not tested with a colour-blind filter. Reduce motion exists. Suggested fix: allow zoom, set a 12px floor with an A, A+ and A++ text-size toggle, pad small targets, add a left or right hand layout (menu to the bottom), and run a colour-blind filter test.
• R2-10 [Low] Phone resilience: save and resume work, but offline and storage are fragile. Evidence: I reloaded mid-combat after playing a card and Continue restored the same hand and energy, which is good. The gaps: no web manifest and no service worker (it cannot be installed or run offline), fonts come from Google Fonts at runtime, and saves live only in localStorage in a single slot, so clearing site data, or the browser evicting storage for sites not added to the home screen, can erase both the run and the banked Nassau silver. Performance is fine on the title screen (407 KB, 18 requests); art used later in a run was not measured. Suggested fix: add a manifest and a service worker that precaches the art, self-host the fonts, request persistent storage, and offer an export or import save code.
• R2-11 [Medium] Combat is dense and hides its math. Evidence: the combat screen at 390x844 carries about 76 words and numbers: three health tracks, silver, powder, rations, kits, four officer slots, weather, phase, enemy hull and crew, a log, and deck and discard counts. The enemy intent is an icon, a number and tiny ship, crew or heart glyphs; the words ("Grapeshot 6, kills crew") only appear if you tap the badge. Real damage also depends on hidden dials (per-act enemy damage multipliers, weather plus or minus 2, Weak cutting 25%, half damage with no powder), and the first Act I sloop opens with a Broadside 11 against 45 hull, with no cue whether 11 is scary. Suggested fix: print the verb under the intent badge, add an incoming-damage preview ("after Block: 4 hull"), and fold supplies and officers into a tray that expands on tap.
• R2-12 [Medium] There are no wow moments; the biggest beats are text. Evidence: the high points of a run are a boss name on a panel, a trophy line and a text end screen. The Hornigold duel is a portrait and a health bar, sometimes for 17 to 22 turns. A prize ship capture, a first sinking, extraction and the final victory all reuse the same card panel and a reward list. Nothing is authored to make a player screenshot it or tell a friend: no boss entrance, no broadside cinematic, no sailing into Nassau at the end. Suggested fix: author three set pieces: a boss intro card for each act, a one-shot cannon volley and boarding charge for kill moments, and an ending sequence sailing into Nassau with the plunder tally.
